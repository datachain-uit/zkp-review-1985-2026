## 1. Rationale and gap

Existing secondary studies on ZKPs are either (a) narrative and non-systematic, (b) focused on a single slice, or (c) systematic but small:

| Study | Scope | Method | Limitation relative to this review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Li & McMillin, *Advances in Computers* 2014 | Classical ZKP theory | Narrative | Pre-SNARK era |
| Morais et al., *SN Appl. Sci.* 2019 | Range proofs | Narrative | One primitive |
| Sun et al., *IEEE Network* 2021 | ZKP in blockchain | Narrative, short | One domain |
| Lavin et al., arXiv 2408.00243 (v2 2026) | Applications, zkSNARK-centric | Narrative + taxonomy | No systematic search/selection |
| Sheybani et al., arXiv 2502.07063 | 25 open-source frameworks | Benchmark survey | Frameworks only |
| Liang et al., USENIX Security 2025 (SoK) | ~40 zk-SNARKs + 11 libraries | SoK | Not systematic; SNARKs only |
| Peng et al., *AI Review* 2026 | zkML, 27 studies | Systematic | One domain |
| Hu et al., *Chinese J. Electronics* 2025 | zk-SNARK HW accelerators | Narrative | One layer of the stack |
| Dominic et al., CITISIA 2022; Pathak-style bibliometrics 2021 | ZKP applications / authentication | Bibliometric, ≤ 330 papers | Small corpus, no synthesis |
| Williams, *CACM* 67(7) 2024 (blog) | ZKP in blockchain | Opinion | Not a survey |

**Gap.** No study systematically maps the *entire* ZKP literature from Goldwasser–Micali–Rackoff (STOC 1985) to 2026 with a reproducible, PRISMA-reported pipeline, nor connects the three layers that practitioners actually traverse: *proof-system construction → systems engineering → application*. This review fills that gap with a two-tier design (§3).

## 2. Research questions

| ID | Question | Tier | Primary output |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | How has ZKP research evolved from 1985 to 2026 in volume, publication venues, research communities, and geography? | Mapping | Growth curves, venue/community map, co-authorship & country networks |
| RQ2 | Which proof-system families and core techniques exist (arithmetization, polynomial commitments, IOPs, folding/accumulation, lookups, recursion, post-quantum constructions), and how do they compare on setup, assumptions, proof size, prover and verifier cost? | Tier 1 coding + Tier 2 SLR | Distribution over time (Tier 1); taxonomy + comparison table (Tier 2) |
| RQ3 | How are ZKPs engineered and accelerated in practice (front-ends/DSLs/compilers, zkVMs, libraries, GPU/FPGA/ASIC, distributed proving), and what performance is reported under which workloads? | Tier 1 coding + Tier 2 SLR | Distribution over time (Tier 1); systems taxonomy + normalized performance table (Tier 2) |
| RQ4 | In which application domains are ZKPs used, which statements are proven, which proof systems are chosen and why, and at what maturity level? | Tier 1 coding + Tier 2 SLR | Domain × proof-system matrix over the whole corpus (Tier 1); statements proven, reasons for choices, maturity (Tier 2) |
| RQ5 | Which topics are emerging or declining, and which open challenges recur across RQ2–RQ4? | Mapping + SLR | Topic trajectories, research agenda |
| CQ | (Cross-cutting question over RQ2–RQ4.) How does soundness fail in practice — under-constrained or buggy circuits, Fiat–Shamir/transcript flaws, trusted-setup compromise, prover side channels, compiler and verifier bugs — and how are such failures detected or prevented (static analysis, fuzzing, formal verification, audits)? | Tier 1 coding + Tier 2 SLR | Failure class × layer × detection-method table; counts; CQ conclusion |

**Cross-cutting question CQ — security and correctness.** Across RQ2–RQ4 we additionally code how soundness can fail in practice: under-constrained or buggy circuits, Fiat–Shamir and transcript implementation flaws, trusted-setup compromise, side channels in provers, and formal verification of circuits, compilers, and verifiers. Because these failures span all three layers, they are answered as one named cross-cutting question (CQ), reported in its own section after RQ4 and before RQ5 (whose agenda draws on it), with the same table → counts → conclusion-box format as the RQs. CQ is not a fourth axis (D1) and does not add a stratum to the Tier 2 selection. *(Superseded by D9: CQ forms a fourth selection stratum.)* *(Changed 2026-11-07 from "lens reported as a subsection", for a consistent one-question-per-section structure.)*

**Why these three axes.** They follow the layered pipeline that both researchers and practitioners traverse when building a ZK application — a statement is compiled into a constraint system, proven by a proving system, and deployed in an application — which is also the decomposition used by recent SoK work (constraint system / proving system / compiler; Liang et al., USENIX Security 2025). The final taxonomy inside each axis is derived from the data with the Nickerson et al. (2013) iterative method, so new sub-categories may emerge during extraction; adding a whole new axis after registration is recorded as an amendment.

## 3. Design: two tiers

```
                 Identification (all sources, 1985–2026)
                               │
                     De-duplication + merge
                               │
   Title/abstract relevance screening (LLM ensemble + human audit, §7)
                               │
             TIER 1: mapping corpus (all relevant works)
   → RQ1, RQ5; abstract-level coding of axis, proof-system family,
     systems layer and application domain for RQ2–RQ4 trends (§6.2a)
                               │
              Quality gate (§6.2) → Tier 2 candidate pool
                               │
          Tier 2 selection rule (§6.2b; target 250–400 works)
                               │
   Full-text eligibility + QA (§6.3) → snowballing (§7, step 4)
                               │
                    TIER 2: deep SLR corpus
             → RQ2, RQ3, RQ4, RQ5 (full-text extraction)
```

Tier 1 answers "what exists and how it moves", including how proof-system families, systems layers and application domains are distributed over time (coded from titles and abstracts); Tier 2 answers "what works, how, and how well" from full texts. Both appear in a single flow diagram with a branch.

**Why Tier 2 is capped (D8).** Comparable systematic reviews in ACM Computing Surveys analyse 26–231 studies in depth in 27–39 pages (`01_protocol/csur_benchmark.md`). The pre-specified quality gate alone admits about 3,200 works, which cannot be synthesised in depth within the page budget by a single author. Tier 2 is therefore drawn from the gate's candidate pool by a selection rule fixed before any full text is read (§6.2b). The breadth that Tier 2 gives up is carried by the Tier 1 coding, which is the review's main novelty in scale.

## 5. Search strategy

### 5.1 Concept block

Single concept (ZKP and its direct technical synonyms). Domain terms are **not** ANDed in, so that Tier 1 is complete; domain classification happens at extraction.

```
"zero knowledge" OR "zero-knowledge" OR "zk-SNARK*" OR zkSNARK* OR "zk-STARK*" OR zkSTARK*
OR "succinct non-interactive argument*" OR "succinct argument*" OR SNARK OR SNARKs
OR "non-interactive argument*" OR NIZK OR NIZKs OR Bulletproof* OR "Sigma protocol*"
OR "interactive oracle proof*" OR "polynomial commitment*" OR "proof-carrying data"
OR zkVM* OR zkEVM* OR "zk-rollup*" OR "zk rollup*" OR zkML OR "ZK proof*" OR ZKP OR ZKPs
OR "proof of knowledge"
OR R1CS OR "rank-1 constraint*" OR "rank-one constraint*" OR "multi-scalar multiplication*"   ← revision 1
```

**Revision 1 (2026-11-02, before screening).** The initial recall test (§5.3) showed that building-block papers can describe ZK proof systems without any ZK term (e.g., "proofs for R1CS", "MSM on GPUs"). Four low-noise building-block terms were added. In Scopus they are executed as a *delta* query (`TITLE-ABS-KEY(new terms) AND NOT TITLE-ABS-KEY(original terms)`, file `02_search/scopus_queries/p3_REVISION1_delta.txt`); in OpenAlex/arXiv as additional per-term queries; in ePrint by the local regular expression. Broader terms (e.g., NTT, "verifiable computation", "identification scheme") were considered and rejected because of their noise; papers they would add are expected to be found by snowballing.

Rationale for non-ZK terms (SNARK, IOP, polynomial commitment, proof-carrying data): these are the building blocks of modern ZK systems and are frequently published without the phrase "zero-knowledge" in the abstract. Records using them are retained only if eligibility criterion IC2 holds.

### 5.3 Search validation (recall test)

A **gold set** of 75 known-relevant papers is listed in `02_search/gold_set.csv`: 52 landmark works (1985–2025; e.g., GMR85, GMW91, Fiat–Shamir86, BFM88, Kilian92, Micali CS-proofs, GGPR13, Pinocchio, Groth16, Zerocash, Bulletproofs, STARK, Ligero, Aurora, Sonic, PLONK, Halo, Libra, Virgo, Nova, Brakedown, Orion, Lasso, Jolt, Binius, zkCNN) and 23 papers from the author's prior reading (`reference` folder; one paper on NTT for homomorphic encryption was not ZKP-related and was left out). The merged search must retrieve ≥ 95 % of the gold set; missed items trigger string revision and are reported.

**Results.** Initial string: 71/75 (94.7 %). Misses: Fiat–Shamir 1986 and Schnorr 1989 (identification-scheme terminology, no ZK term in title or abstract), Micali 2000 (CS proofs) and LaBRADOR (R1CS terminology). Revision 1 recovered LaBRADOR: **72/75 (96.0 %)**, meeting the 95 % target. The three classic papers that no reasonable search term retrieves are added as "records identified from other methods" (`02_search/other_sources.csv`) and are also expected to be found by snowballing; all 75 gold papers are in the screening corpus. Note: an MSM acceleration paper (Chen et al. 2024) first appeared as a miss because its gold-set title had been copied from PDF metadata ("MSM" instead of "Multi-Scalar Multiplication"); it was in fact retrieved by the initial Scopus search. The "multi-scalar multiplication" term added in revision 1 is retained as a building-block term for the ZK proving kernel. Figures: `02_search/prisma_identification.json`, `gold_recall.csv`.

## 6. Eligibility criteria

### 6.1 Tier 1 (relevance)

| Code | Inclusion criterion |
|---|---|
| IC1 | The work's primary subject is a zero-knowledge proof/argument, or a succinct argument / IOP / polynomial commitment / proof-carrying-data scheme designed or used for ZK; **or** it applies ZKPs as a core mechanism of its contribution. |
| IC2 | Non-ZK succinct arguments and their building blocks (SNARKs without ZK, IOPs, PCP-based arguments, polynomial commitments, lookup arguments, folding/accumulation, recursion, arithmetization such as R1CS) are included, because they form the proof-system layer of ZK systems. Interactive proofs, PCPs and complexity results *without* the zero-knowledge property are not (EC2). |
| IC3 | Published 1985–2026 (inclusive) as a journal article, conference/workshop paper, book chapter, ePrint/arXiv preprint, or thesis (theses: Tier 1 only). |
| IC4 | Written in English, with an accessible title and abstract. |

| Code | Exclusion criterion |
|---|---|
| EC1 | "Zero-knowledge" in the non-cryptographic sense (zero-knowledge encryption marketing, "zero-knowledge" storage, zero-shot learning confusion, chemistry/biology acronyms). |
| EC2 | ZKPs appear only as background, related work, an alternative or future work, not as a mechanism of the contribution; or the work concerns interactive/probabilistic proofs or complexity without the zero-knowledge property; or MSM/NTT acceleration without a ZKP motivation; or verifiable computation without a proof system (detailed rules: `03_screening/screening_guide.md`). |
| EC3 | Editorials, prefaces, proceedings front matter, patents, slides, blog posts, retracted papers. |
| EC4 | Duplicates: superseded preprints are merged into the published version (the preprint's date is kept as "first appearance"). |
| EC5 | Deposits only on general-purpose repositories (Zenodo, Figshare, SSRN, Research Square, HAL, OSF Preprints, TechRxiv, Preprints.org, …) with no version in an indexed venue, ePrint or arXiv (§4). |

### 6.1a Metadata-based pre-screening (automation, reported in PRISMA as "records removed before screening")

Applied to whole de-duplicated works, never to single records: a work is removed only if *every* record of it meets the rule.
- **AUTO-EC3 (record type):** OpenAlex types dataset, software, paratext, erratum, peer-review, standard, editorial, book-review, reference-entry, conference-abstract, retraction; Scopus types Conference review (proceedings front matter), Erratum, Editorial, Retracted.
- **AUTO-SCOPE / EC5:** all records of the work are deposits on general-purpose repositories (§4).
- **Language flag (not an exclusion):** works known only from OpenAlex whose language metadata is not English are flagged; IC4 is decided at screening because automatic language detection is unreliable on short titles.
- A random sample of 100 auto-excluded works was inspected by the LLM assistant (0 false exclusions, `02_search/qa_checks.md`) and is confirmed by R1 before registration; the false-exclusion rate is reported.

### 6.2 Tier 2 quality gate (pre-full-text)

A Tier 1 record enters Tier 2 if **any** of the following holds:

- **Q-Venue:** published in a venue ranked CORE A*/A, CCF A/B, or a journal in SJR Q1 (Computer Science / Mathematics) in the publication year (closest available ranking).
- **Q-Impact:** preprint or lower-ranked venue whose field- and age-normalised citation count is in the top 10 % of its publication-year cohort within the Tier 1 corpus (computed from OpenAlex), or ≥ 50 total citations.
- **Q-Seminal:** gold-set paper or cited by ≥ 3 included secondary studies (consensus seminal works).

Records published in 2025–2026 are evaluated on Q-Venue only, with a recency exception for ePrint papers already accepted at A*/A venues (see D9 for a pending change for 2025).

*Implementation (2026-11-07, script `04_quality/scripts/tier2_gate.py`).* CORE: editions 2008, ERA 2010, 2013, 2014, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2021 and ICORE 2026 (the ICORE 2023 export could not be retrieved; the portal returned the 2026 edition); for each paper the closest edition that lists the conference is used. Conference names come from Scopus (OpenAlex venue strings as fallback) and are matched by acronym and normalised title; workshops and companion volumes never inherit the rank of the main conference. SJR: files for 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2024 (closest year); "Q1" means Q1 in at least one Computer Science or Mathematics category of a journal (see D9). CCF: OpenAlex source listings (ccf-a / ccf-b). Citations: OpenAlex counts; for ePrint/arXiv-only works absent from OpenAlex the title is looked up in OpenAlex (title similarity ≥ 0.92; `fetch_preprint_citations.py`). Secondary studies for Q-Seminal: included works of type review, or with survey / systematic review / SoK / overview in the title.

### 6.2a Tier 1 abstract-level coding

Every Tier 1 work is coded from its title, abstract and keywords by an LLM with a fixed prompt and codebook derived from §8: axis (construction / building block / systems & acceleration / application / attack-or-bug / formal verification / survey), proof-system family, systems layer and platform, application domain, and whether the security & correctness lens applies. Codes are labelled "abstract-level" wherever reported. **Validation:** R1 codes a stratified random sample of 200 works blind to the LLM codes; per-field accuracy and Cohen's κ are reported, and a field is used for inference only if κ ≥ 0.6 (otherwise it is reported descriptively with this caveat or merged into coarser categories). Prompt, model, date and raw codes are archived. This coding is done **before** the Tier 2 selection, because the selection is stratified by axis.

### 6.2b Tier 2 selection (from the quality-gate pool)

Target size 250–400 works. The rule is fixed by amendment before any full text is read and is chosen on feasibility and coverage, never on the content of results. Constraints: (i) all Q-Seminal works are kept; (ii) the selection is stratified by the Tier 1 axis so that constructions, systems & acceleration and applications are all represented — application papers rarely appear in A*-ranked venues, so a venue-only rule would bias Tier 2 towards constructions; (iii) within a stratum, works are ordered by a pre-stated priority (venue rank, then age-normalised citations), with a quota for 2025–2026 so that recent work is not lost to citation lag. Candidate rules and their counts are reported, together with the rule adopted (D9).

### 6.3 Tier 2 quality assessment (full text)

Each Tier 2 paper is scored 0 / 0.5 / 1 on:

| QA | Criterion |
|---|---|
| QA1 | Problem and contribution are clearly stated. |
| QA2 | Security model and assumptions are explicit (for constructions) / threat model is explicit (for applications). |
| QA3 | Security claim is proven, or relies on a cited proven scheme. |
| QA4 | Complexity or performance is reported (asymptotic and/or empirical). |
| QA5 | Empirical evaluation states hardware, parameters, circuit size, and baselines (if empirical). |
| QA6 | Artifact (code/benchmark) is publicly available. |
| QA7 | Limitations are discussed. |
| QA8 | Comparison with relevant prior work is fair and current. |

Papers scoring < 4.0 / 8 are kept in Tier 1 but excluded from Tier 2 synthesis (reported in the flow diagram). QA scores are reported in the supplement and used for sensitivity analysis.

## 7. Selection process

1. **De-duplication** (`merge_dedup.py`): records are clustered into works by (i) identical normalized DOI, (ii) identical normalized title (≥ 25 characters, ignoring case, punctuation, markup and "(extended abstract)") with publication years within ±1, or any year gap when one record is a preprint, and (iii) near-identical titles (similarity ≥ 0.94, same first 20 characters, years within ±1). Conference and journal versions are merged into one work when they share a DOI or a title-identical preprint (e.g., Ligero: CCS 2017 and its 2023 journal version, both linked to the same ePrint entry); otherwise they are kept as separate *reports* and linked to the same *study* at full-text stage. The displayed title is the longest complete title in the cluster (OpenAlex sometimes truncates titles at the colon). Each work receives a stable identifier derived from its source identifiers, so that re-running the merge after adding sources does not renumber screened records. A random sample of merged clusters and all clusters whose member titles differ (similarity < 0.9) are checked manually for false merges (result in `02_search/qa_checks.md`).
2. **Title/abstract screening (single-author design; as executed — option D, decision D7).**
   - **History.** v0.2/v0.3 planned that R1 screens every record with an LLM as second screener (options B/C). A blind 200-record pilot (100 records labelled by R1) showed R1's blind labels to be the limiting factor (sensitivity 0.761 against adjudicated labels), and full human screening of ≈ 16,000 records was estimated at ≈ 180 h. Before any validation run, acceptance criteria for an LLM-ensemble design were written and time-stamped (`03_screening/pilot/option_D_prespecification.md`, 2026-11-02T08:16Z). Pilot figures for all designs are reported.
   - **Screeners.** Two primary LLM screeners (claude-haiku-5-5 and claude-sonnet-5-5) label every record independently with the fixed prompt v1 (`03_screening/llm_screening_prompt_v1.md`), run through Claude Code in headless mode (tools disabled, effort "low", 20 records per call). A third screener (claude-opus-5-5, effort "low") labels a record when the primaries disagree on relevance, either says U, either excludes with confidence < 0.8, or an EC5 label is given to an ePrint/arXiv record (invalid by definition). Raw labels, model identifiers, dates and token usage are archived (`03_screening/llm_runs/`).
   - **Validation before use.** On 249 records (pilot records plus the 75 gold-set papers mixed with 75 random records; screeners not told) the exact configuration met the pre-specified criteria: ensemble gold-set sensitivity 75/75, Haiku–Sonnet κ = 0.82 on the pilot, no clear-cut exclusion of a relevant record (`option_D_validation_results.md`).
   - **Decision rule** (`consolidate_screening.py`). Agreement of the primaries without any routing condition → final. Routed records → 2-of-3 majority on relevance (I/U vs E). Third-screener U → R1, who decides with the Vietnamese reading aid. Third-screener self-reported confidence is not used as a gate, because at effort low it is poorly calibrated (mostly 0.5–0.75).
   - **Blind human audit** (`human_review_tools.py`, seed 20261103). 400 records drawn at random from all exclusions (pre-specified criterion 4: one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson upper bound of the false-exclusion rate ≤ 2 %) plus an extra stratum of 100 majority exclusions, shown to R1 in random order without any model label.
   - **Results.** 16,161 works screened; Haiku–Sonnet agreement 0.945, κ = 0.883; 2,348 (14.5 %) routed to the third screener; 124 decided by R1 (17 included). Audit: 1/400 false exclusions (upper bound 1.18 %, criterion met); extra stratum 2/100 (upper bound 6.2 %), both majority exclusions in which one primary screener had said I. All three were reinstated. A conservative recheck, fixed before it was run, re-screened the 236 majority exclusions with a primary I label using claude-opus-5-5 at effort "high"; an I or U label reinstated the work (the recheck can only add): 51 reinstated. **Final: 10,190 included, 5,971 excluded** (`03_screening/screening_final.csv`). All 75 gold-set papers are included. *(Updated 2026-11-20 by D20: after the blind repeat labelling and its adjudication, 23 of the 124 unsure works are included, the audit counts are 3/400 (upper bound 1.93 %, criterion met) and 4/100 (8.9 %), and screening yields 10,200 included and 5,961 excluded works.)*
   - **Intra-rater reliability (R1).** R1 re-labels a random 10 % of the 624 human decisions (audit and unresolved records) ≥ 14 days later, blind to the first labels; test–retest κ is reported.
   - **External human calibration (if feasible):** a colleague screens a 100-record calibration sample; κ reported. If not feasible, this is stated as a limitation.
   - **Reading aid (non-native reviewer).** R1 is not a native English speaker. R1's screening pages show, under each English title and abstract, a Vietnamese machine translation and a 1–2-sentence neutral Vietnamese summary, produced by separate LLM instances instructed to translate faithfully and never to comment on relevance, criteria or quality; they never see screener labels. The English original remains the basis of every decision (`03_screening/translation_instructions.md`).

3. **Full-text eligibility + QA:** R1 decides; the LLM produces independent QA scores and extraction drafts for every Tier 2 paper; disagreements are adjudicated by R1, and human–LLM agreement on QA1–QA8 is reported. R1 re-scores a 10 % sample after ≥ 14 days (intra-rater κ).
4. **Snowballing:** one round backward + forward on the Tier 2 set (Wohlin 2014) using OpenAlex references and citations; candidates are screened with the same LLM-ensemble pipeline and decision rule (§7, step 2), pass the quality gate, and enter Tier 2 under the same selection rule (§6.2b). Procedure details, including the pre-filter and its miss-rate check: D12.

**Use of AI tools** (disclosed per ACM policy and reported following RAISE): search-string drafting and scripting; Vietnamese reading aid; LLM-ensemble title/abstract screening, which made the final decision for records on which the ensemble agreed, validated and audited as in step 2; abstract-level coding of Tier 1, validated on a human-coded sample (§6.2a); QA scores and extraction drafts for Tier 2, each verified by R1. The author remains responsible for every decision. Prompts, model versions, dates, token usage and agreement statistics are reported in the supplement.

## 8. Data extraction (Tier 2)

Form: `04_extraction/extraction_form.csv`. Fields:

## 9. Synthesis and analysis

- **RQ1 (bibliometrics):** annual production and CAGR; venue/community distribution (crypto, security, systems/architecture, networking, AI, application domains); country and institution collaboration networks; co-citation clusters (VOSviewer / bibliometrix-R).
- **RQ5 (trend mining):** keyword co-occurrence over time windows; BERTopic topic model on titles+abstracts with dynamic topics per year; burst detection (Kleinberg) on author keywords; emerging-topic definition = topic share growth > 2× over the last three years.
- **RQ2–RQ4, breadth (Tier 1 abstract-level codes):** distributions and trends of axis, proof-system family, systems layer and application domain over 1985–2026; cross-tabulations (domain × proof-system family) on the whole corpus, reported as abstract-level with the validation figures of §6.2a.
- **RQ2–RQ4, depth (Tier 2, qualitative + quantitative synthesis):** taxonomy development by iterative coding (Nickerson et al. 2013 method), comparison tables, cross-tabulations (domain × proof system, platform × kernel), and normalized performance summaries (speed-ups reported relative to a common baseline where possible; heterogeneity explicitly reported — no meta-analysis of performance numbers).
- **Validity threats** (construct, internal, external, conclusion; Wohlin et al.) discussed in a dedicated section.
